Run a Free Echo Test Online in Your Browser

The echo test takes about a minute. It listens to your voice, shows what the microphone hears as it happens, and flags repeated sound, so echo detection is something you can see rather than guess. Use it before a call or a meeting to hear your setup the way other people do. For the answer to what microphone do i have, the tool reads your audio input devices without any software.

Allow Microphone Access

Press the start button and your browser asks for microphone permission. Choose Allow for the input device you want to test. If the prompt never appears, open the site settings for this page and switch the device on. A headset or webcam can be the default input, so pick the right one before you speak.

Speak and Watch Echo Diagnostics

Talk at a normal volume. The oscilloscope draws your waveform live, while the spectrum analyzer splits the sound into bass, midrange and treble. The echo diagnostics panel then scores six things: signal strength, echo loop, feedback risk, reverb tail, signal clarity and noise floor. A clean setup shows a low noise floor and a clarity score that stays high while you speak. Background noise or a noisy fan lowers that score first.

Try Echo Playback With a Delay Slider

Switch on echo playback to hear your voice returned after a delay. Drag the delay slider from 50 ms up to 3 s: short delays sound like a metallic ring, while anything past 100 ms is a clear repeat. Raise the feedback intensity and the repeat loops. The wet mix sets how loud the echo is against the dry signal. Wear headphones for this step, because open speakers turn the playback into real feedback. Make a short recording, then listen to the playback to judge the result.

What a Microphone Test Online Detects

A good microphone test online does more than show a moving bar. It separates three problems that sound alike to the listener: echo, feedback and reverb. Each has a different cause, and each needs a different fix on the speaker, microphone or room side.

What Is Microphone Echo?

Echo happens when your voice is captured, played from another person's speakers, picked up by their microphone and returned to you late. Omnidirectional microphones, such as most laptop mics, collect speaker bleed from every side, so they suffer most. Echo in a meeting is usually a delay of 100 ms or more, long enough to hear the repeat as a separate sound.

Audio Feedback Loop Detection

A feedback loop forms when the microphone hears the speakers and the speakers replay what it hears, round and round, until a squeal or howl appears. The correlation graph in this tool spikes while the loop is still too quiet to hear. Turning off input monitoring in your recording software also matters, because that setting routes the microphone straight to the speakers on purpose.

Room Reverb and Acoustic Reflection

Reverb is the blur left when sound bounces off desks, windows and other hard surfaces and re-enters the microphone a few milliseconds late. Unlike an echo, it does not repeat cleanly; it smears syllables together. The echo correlation graph compares the live signal with a delayed copy of itself using cross-correlation, so reflections show up as bumps while a quiet room stays flat.

Hearing Test: 125 Hz to 20 kHz

The built-in hearing test plays twelve sine wave tones from 125 Hz to 20 kHz, and it backs up the echo results: a tone that sounds doubled or sustained means the room is reflecting sound that can feed an echo, a sign to treat the space before you record. The highest tone you can still hear also marks your hearing range, which for most adults runs to about 18 kHz. The microphone visualizer beside it shows each tone arriving, so you can confirm that both speaker and microphone handle that frequency. A speaker whose frequency response drops off near 16 kHz will look deaf to the top tones, and the frequency range your microphone captures is worth checking the same way.

Works on Every Device and Browser

The test runs in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS. A built-in laptop mic, a USB headset or a podcast microphone all work, and so does a streaming setup, as long as the browser can see the device. Good audio quality starts with a signal the browser can actually read.

Echo Detection at a Home Desk: Timing a Wall Reflection

I'm recording a voice-over tomorrow in a spare room, and I want to know whether the bare wall behind my screen will show up as echo. I run the test with my USB microphone, headphones on, and clap once. The correlation graph shows a small bump right after the clap, while the clarity score stays high, so something is reflecting.

To judge it, I measure the room: the microphone sits 1.9 m from the wall behind it. Sound travels at roughly 343 m/s, and the reflection covers the distance twice, so the delay is t = 2d / c = (2 × 1.9) / 343 ≈ 11.1 ms. That is far below the 50 ms mark where reverb blurs into a distinct repeat, so this is reverb, not echo.

Mic to wallReflection delayWhat you hear
1.9 m11.1 msReverb, a slightly boxy tone
3.4 m19.8 msReverb, a longer tail
17.2 m100 msA clear separate echo

The sensitivity rerun is the useful part: I would need a wall about 17.2 m away for a true 100 ms echo, so no desk setup produces one by reflection alone. If a listener hears a distinct repeat, it comes from the call path, not my room. The number to watch is therefore the clarity score, not the delay. My decision: hang a thick blanket behind the microphone, rerun the test, and aim for a flatter correlation line before I record. One caveat is that this arithmetic only covers the first bounce; a small, hard-walled room adds many reflections, and the simple formula will understate how muddy it sounds.

How to Fix Echo and Feedback After an Echo Detector Check

Once the check points to a cause, fix that cause instead of lowering every setting. In video calls the conferencing app can only cancel what it can predict, so the physical setup comes first.

Use Headphones and Lower Your Speaker Volume

Use headphones first: they separate the speakers from the microphone and remove the feedback path completely. If you must use laptop speakers, lower the speaker volume to 50-70% and move the microphone 30-60 cm away from them. In a group call, mute yourself while others talk.

Enable Acoustic Echo Cancellation in Your App

Zoom, Teams, Google Meet and Discord all include echo cancellation, so turn it on in each app's audio settings and set noise suppression to high where offered. If you also run recording software, disable its input monitoring, and turn off Windows sound effects that add reverb. Then run the echo and feedback detector again. If the echo loop and feedback risk both read clear, the remaining echo is coming from the other side of the call.

Soften the Room With a Directional Microphone

A cardioid pickup pattern hears mostly what sits in front of it, so a directional microphone rejects speaker sound and room reflections that an omnidirectional laptop mic would collect. Add soft furnishings such as rugs, curtains and bookshelves, or hang a thick blanket or acoustic panels behind the microphone to cut the reverb you saw in the diagnostics.

Troubleshooting Your Online Mic Tester

If the waveform stays flat, the mic tester is not receiving a signal. Run a quick mic check of the common causes below before you assume the hardware is broken, because a microphone tester can only report what the browser lets it hear.

Browser Permission, Drivers and Other Apps

Start with browser permission: a blocked site shows no signal at all. Next, close Zoom, Discord or any app that may hold the microphone, confirm the device is enabled in your operating system's privacy settings, update the drivers, and reseat the cable or try another USB port. If another computer picks up the same mic, the hardware is fine and the cause is settings or drivers.

Private by Design: Nothing Leaves Your Device

Every echo analysis on this page runs through the Web Audio API inside your browser, so your voice is processed in memory on your device and never uploaded. It stays private: no account, no stored recording and nothing for a server to keep.

Echo Test — Frequently Asked Questions

Is this online echo test free?

Yes. It needs no account, no download and no payment, and it runs in your browser.

Why do I hear my own voice echoing on a call?

Usually another participant is using speakers without echo cancellation, so your voice plays out of their speakers, re-enters their microphone and returns to you late. Ask them to use headphones or enable echo cancellation.

What is the difference between echo and reverb?

Echo is a distinct repeat that arrives roughly 100 ms or later. Reverb is many short reflections, each under about 50 ms, that blur together as the sound decays.

How do I stop audio feedback?

Use headphones, lower the speaker volume, move the microphone away from the speakers and mute yourself when you are not talking.

Does the test record or upload my voice?

No. All audio is processed locally in your browser's memory on your own device, so nothing is uploaded or stored.

Why does the tool say clipping risk?

Input gain above about 220% with typical speech peaks pushes the signal past full scale, which distorts the recording. Lower the gain until the result shows headroom.

Do I need headphones when I use echo playback?

Yes. Without headphones, the speakers feed the delayed sound back into the microphone and create real feedback.
