Wondering whether it is time to check your hearing? This free online hearing test is a quick way to find out how well you or a loved one hear, and the whole hearing screener takes about three minutes. Run the hearing test online from your own device, then use the result to decide what to do next.

How the Free Online Hearing Test Works

Press start, check the sound, and follow the prompts. The 3-minute hearing test plays a series of tones, and you respond each time you hear one. The on-screen prompts then show what you heard, with recommendations and next steps. For speaker calibration or tinnitus matching, the tone generator gives you precise frequency control.

Headphones or Device Speakers

Choose headphones when you can. Unlike device speakers, they test your right and left ears separately, which gives more accurate results. Set the volume to the highest level that stays comfortable, and if you wear hearing aids, remove them before you begin.

Preparing for a Hearing Test Online

Take the test in the comfort of your home, with the TV off and no conversations nearby, so nothing competes with the softest tones.

Find a Quiet Place

A quiet place matters most: background noise hides faint tones and makes your hearing look worse than it is. Close windows, silence phone alerts and sit still while the tones play.

Hearing Test Results by Hearing Loss Level

The World Health Organization grades hearing loss by the softest sound you hear, measured in decibels (dB). Under 20 dB is normal, 20-34 dB is mild hearing loss, 35-49 dB is moderate, 50-64 dB is moderately severe, 65-79 dB is severe, and 80 dB or more is profound. Moderate or worse in the better ear counts as disabling hearing loss.

Take an example listener who hears tones at 20, 25, 20, 30 and 35 dB in the right ear, averaging 26 dB (mild), and at 35, 40, 45, 50 and 55 dB in the left, averaging 45 dB (moderate). The gap between ears is the finding to raise, because only a professional can confirm the type and severity or give a diagnosis.

Grading Your Father's Online Hearing Test Result

My father keeps turning the television up, so the decision I am making is simple: does he book an appointment now or retest first? I sit him in the spare room with headphones, run the online hearing test, and write down the softest tone he catches at five pitches. Right ear: 20, 20, 25, 45 and 60 dB. Left ear: 25, 30, 35, 55 and 70 dB.

The five-tone averages are 170 ÷ 5 = 34 dB (right) and 215 ÷ 5 = 43 dB (left). On the World Health Organization bands that makes the right ear mild (20-34 dB) and the left ear moderate (35-49 dB). Because the better ear is the right one, he does not meet the WHO disabling hearing loss definition, but he is a single decibel from it.

That one decibel is why I rerun the arithmetic before trusting the grade:

ScenarioRight earLeft ear
All five tones34 dB43 dB
Drop the 8 kHz tone27.5 dB36.25 dB
Noise adds 5 dB to every tone39 dB48 dB

The row to watch is the last one. A noisy room, or a loose headphone cup, would push his right ear into the moderate band without his hearing changing at all. In my judgement, the steep high-pitch drop-off matters more than the grade: his 8 kHz readings are 60 and 70 dB, so the averages overstate how he copes with low-pitched speech and understate how much he misses consonants.

The next action is specific: retest tomorrow in a closed room, and if the left ear still averages 35 dB or more, book a hearing care professional, because an online screen cannot say why the two sides differ.

Signs of Hearing Loss a Hearing Screener Can Flag Early

Catching changes early makes them easier to treat. Common signs of hearing loss include: Use a sound level meter online to check whether your recording environment is quiet enough for clean audio.

  • Difficulty hearing other people, especially on the phone
  • Asking people to repeat themselves
  • Turning up the volume on the radio or television
  • Listening fatigue after a day of concentrating

Left alone, mild hearing loss can raise dementia risk and leave you feeling isolated. Anxiety and strain on mental health and well-being often follow.

Online Hearing Test Limits Versus an In-Person Test

This check is not a substitute for a professional hearing test, and it is not a medical diagnosis. Only an in-person test can establish the cause of what you hear.

When to See a Hearing Care Professional

Book a hearing care professional if the result flags a concern or you notice the signs above. They can fit hearing aids if needed and guide your next steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this replace a professional hearing exam?

No. This is a screening tool, not a clinical audiological examination. A professional audiogram uses calibrated equipment in a sound-treated booth and measures your hearing threshold in decibels at each frequency — information this browser test cannot provide. If the results concern you, or if you notice a significant left/right difference, see a licensed audiologist for a full evaluation.

Why do I need headphones?

Headphones allow the test to play each frequency to one ear at a time and block out background noise that would otherwise mask the softest test tones. Laptop speakers and phone speakers mix both channels together and pick up room echo, making left/right comparison impossible and low-frequency results unreliable.

What does it mean if I can't hear high frequencies?

Not hearing frequencies above 14,000–16,000 Hz is common in adults over 30 and is usually normal age-related decline. If you cannot hear above 10,000 Hz, or if you notice a significant difference between your left and right ear, consult an audiologist. Loss of speech-range frequencies (500 Hz–4 kHz) is more clinically significant and warrants prompt evaluation.

What is an audiogram?

An audiogram is a chart produced by a clinical hearing test showing the softest sound (in decibels, dBHL) you can detect at each tested frequency. It maps your hearing thresholds across the speech-critical range (250 Hz–8,000 Hz). This online hearing test produces a simplified version — a frequency range display — rather than a full audiogram with dB measurements.

How accurate is an online hearing test?

Online hearing tests are approximate screening tools. Accuracy depends heavily on your headphones, your room noise level, and volume calibration. Studies suggest consumer-grade online tests can estimate hearing thresholds within roughly 10–15 dB of clinical measurements under good conditions. They are best used to track changes over time and to flag potential problems — not to diagnose hearing loss.

Why are very low frequencies (below 40 Hz) hard to hear on this test?

Most headphones and earbuds cannot reproduce frequencies below 40–50 Hz accurately due to the physical size of their drivers. Even if your hearing is perfect at those frequencies, your headphones may not be able to generate the sound. If you want to test bass-range response, use a full-size over-ear headphone or a dedicated subwoofer test with the bass test tool.

What should I do if my results show a left/right difference?

A consistent difference between ears — where one ear clearly hears higher frequencies than the other — is called asymmetric hearing loss and warrants professional evaluation. It can indicate wax buildup on one side, a middle ear problem, or (rarely) more serious conditions. Use our audio latency test to rule out equipment-side issues before concluding the difference is in your hearing.
