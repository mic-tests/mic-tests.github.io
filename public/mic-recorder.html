This online mic recorder is a free, online tool that lets you record your voice online from any browser, pause and resume, then download an MP3 file. Use it as a voice recorder for voice notes, narration or podcast drafts, and export the audio in one click.

How to Record Your Voice Online in Your Browser

Here is how to use this recorder to record audio: press start recording and speak. The level meter, elapsed timer and live waveform show your sound and speech reaching the recording tool, and the voice recorder works on a computer, smartphone or any other device with a microphone. The tool tells you what microphone do i have — including device name, sample rate, and audio processing settings.

Allow Microphone Access First

When the permission dialog appears, allow microphone access. If you blocked it earlier, click the icon in the address bar and choose Allow in Chrome or Firefox, then check the Windows or macOS settings. Pick another microphone from the dropdown if you have several, and lower the volume or echo if the level clips.

Pause and Resume, Then Stop Recording

Step one is to speak at a steady distance. Use pause and resume whenever you need a breath, and hit stop recording when the take is done. Each pause keeps the same recording instead of starting a new file.

Preview, Then Download MP3

Preview the take, then export it. Press download to save the MP3 on your device; the export happens locally in your browser, so the MP3 file is ready in seconds.

Why This Free Voice Recorder Works for Everyone

A free voice recorder should not ask for an account or a sign-up. This one is free, and the high-quality sound suits interviews, dictation, singing, music or video narration. Podcasts start the same way: record a clean take with this tool and download it for your host, so listeners get the episode through an RSS feed.

Privacy and Local Processing

Your privacy is protected because the audio stays local to the browser. Nothing reaches our servers, so there is no storage to secure or encrypted upload to worry about; close the tab and the working copy disappears.

Podcast-Ready MP3 at 128 kbps

This recorder exports an MP3 at a 128 kbps bitrate, a podcast standard that weighs about 0.96 MB per minute. A WAV file is far bigger. Normalize loudness in your editor if the platform you publish to asks for it.

Voice Recording Cleanup and Audio Editor Tools

A short voice recording rarely needs a full audio editor. Basic editing covers the cleanup most takes need: use noise reduction against background noise, then edit and trim the ends before you share it. An app is optional.

Silence Trimming Cuts Dead Air

Silence trimming in this recorder removes the quiet gaps at the start and end of your take, so you trim less by hand.

Crop Takes with an Audio Trimmer

An audio trimmer crops the recorded take to the part you want before download. Trim, preview again and export.

Sizing a 17-Minute Interview with the Online Mic Recorder

I have a 17 minute 40 second interview to email to an editor, and the question is whether the file will fit. I open the online voice recorder, allow the microphone, and keep my peaks near -9 dB on the meter, the middle of the target zone above. The export lands as a 128 kbps MP3.

The size comes straight from the bitrate: 128,000 bits per second is 16,000 bytes per second, and 1,060 seconds of audio gives 16.96 MB. That sits under Gmail's 25 MB attachment limit, so the decision is simple: attach it directly rather than upload it somewhere.

The sensitivity rerun is the useful part. At 192 kbps the same take weighs 25.44 MB, over the limit before any email overhead. Email also encodes attachments with base64, which adds about a third, so the 25 MB cap fits roughly 18.75 MB of raw audio: at the recorder's 128 kbps export, about 19 minutes 32 seconds. The elapsed timer read 17:40, so the take clears it by under two minutes.

My judgement call: the length matters more than the bitrate here. A second interview running 22 minutes would need a share link or a trim first, since speech at 128 kbps already sounds clean. I recomputed the 1,060-second figure by hand from the timer to confirm it before sending.

Transcribe an Audio Recording to Text

To transcribe a take, export the MP3 and run it through speech to text. That transcription gives you text transcripts, and a subtitle or caption file is built from them. The speech recorder itself only captures sound.

Share a Link or Embed Your Voice Notes

Once the file is exported, share a link to it or embed the voice notes in a page. Keep the audio on your side until you choose to publish.

Fix Online Mic Recorder Problems

If the online voice recorder stays silent, treat it like any audio recorder or voice memo app and confirm the browser has the right input selected. Watch the maximum record time and recording length too: a long take means a bigger file. Use a background noise analyzer to check your recording environment before a session.

Supported Browsers and Devices

The recommended browsers are Chrome, Firefox and Safari. An iPhone running iOS needs Safari, and an Android phone works in Chrome.

Online Mic Recorder — Frequently Asked Questions

Does the online mic recorder work on mobile phones?

Yes — the tool works on iOS Safari (version 14.3+) and Android Chrome. On iOS, you must use Safari; Chrome for iOS does not support the MediaRecorder API. On Android, Chrome, Firefox, and Edge all support browser-based recording. Note that mobile microphones typically have higher noise floors and more aggressive noise cancellation than desktop microphones, which may affect recording quality.

Why does my recording sound muffled or very quiet?

Common causes: the microphone input volume in OS settings is set too low (check Sound settings → Input level), a noise cancellation or microphone boost setting is interfering, or you are positioned too far from the microphone. Also check that the correct microphone is selected in the device dropdown — some laptops have multiple microphone inputs (built-in mic, webcam mic, headset mic) and the wrong one may be selected.

Is there a maximum recording length?

There is no hard time limit — the tool records until you click Stop. However, long recordings accumulate in your browser's memory. Recordings above 30–60 minutes may cause memory pressure on devices with limited RAM, particularly on older phones or tablets. For very long recordings (hours), a dedicated desktop recording application is more appropriate.

Why does the waveform not move when I click Record?

This usually means the browser did not receive microphone audio. Check: (1) microphone permission was granted in the browser — look for the microphone icon in the address bar; (2) the correct microphone is selected in the dropdown; (3) your microphone is physically connected and not muted at the hardware level (some headsets have an inline mute button). If the permission was previously denied, reset it in the browser's site settings.

Can I record system audio (computer sounds) as well as my microphone?

Browser-based mic recorders can only capture microphone input — they cannot record system audio (music playing, game sound, etc.) due to browser security restrictions. To record system audio alongside your microphone, use a desktop screen recorder or a virtual audio cable routing tool. The browser MediaRecorder API is limited to microphone streams for privacy reasons.

What browsers support the online voice recorder?

The tool works in any browser that supports the MediaRecorder API: Chrome 49+, Firefox 25+, Safari 14.3+, and Edge 79+. Internet Explorer is not supported. For the best audio quality and most consistent behaviour, use the latest version of Chrome or Firefox on a desktop or laptop. If the record button is unresponsive, update your browser — older versions may lack the required Web Audio API features.
