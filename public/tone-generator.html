Need a clean test sound without installing anything? This free tone generator plays a pure tone in your browser, so you can set a frequency in Hz and hear that audio signal at once. Use it to check your hearing, tune a string or test a speaker, and keep the tones short while you do.

How to Use This Online Tone Generator

  • Press play and start at a low volume.
  • Move the slider to your frequency.
  • Wear headphones, or place speakers at ear height.
  • Choose a waveform, then press stop when you finish.

Set the Frequency with the Slider

Drag the slider anywhere from 1 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Fine-grained steps let you land on 1,000 Hz, a common reference level, or on 20 Hz, where deep bass fades out. To move an octave, halve or double the value: 440 Hz becomes 220 Hz or 880 Hz. An audio latency test shows whether your setup introduces noticeable lag for recording or live use.

Pick a Sine Wave, Square Wave, Sawtooth or Triangle

The pure shape is the cleanest waveform. A square wave, sawtooth or triangle adds overtones that change the sound character. The real-time visualization draws each waveform on an oscilloscope, and the 44.1kHz sample rate keeps it smooth.

Finding a 37 Hz Room Mode with a Sine Wave Generator

My desk speakers sound boomy on one bass note and thin on the next, and I want to know whether the room is to blame. The room is 4.62 m long, so I work out where its first lengthwise resonance should fall before touching the slider: speed of sound divided by twice the length. At 20 °C sound travels at about 343.4 m/s, so 343.4 ÷ (2 × 4.62) gives 37.17 Hz, with repeats at 74.33 Hz and 111.50 Hz.

Then I open the sine wave generator, pick the sine wave so no overtones muddy the test, and set the volume low. I step the frequency through 30, 34, 37, 40 and 45 Hz while sitting in my usual chair. The loudest swell lands at 37 Hz, and a second bump at 74 Hz confirms the pattern. What stands out is that the chair sits 2.31 m from the back wall, exactly half the room length, where a first mode is strongest.

The sensitivity rerun is short: a hot afternoon moves the mode about 0.5 Hz, so I repeat the 37 Hz step in the generator at 38 Hz as well. Against the 20 Hz lower edge of human hearing, 37 Hz is comfortably audible, so this is a real problem, not a curiosity.

My next step is concrete: slide the chair 40 cm toward the back wall, then re-run the sweep at 30, 34, 37, 40 and 45 Hz and compare the swell. One caveat keeps me honest: if the dip stays put when I move the speaker, the driver's own roll-off may be the cause, and laptop speakers cannot play 37 Hz at all. The number to watch is the first mode, because it carries the most energy.

What a Sound Generator Is Used For

In classrooms, students run science experiments with it, and audio engineers rely on it for equipment testing and research.

Hearing Test and Tinnitus Matching

A hearing test starts mid-range and works outward to find the top of your hearing range. To match a tinnitus pitch with it, try 7,350 Hz, then check 3,675 Hz and 14,700 Hz, one octave away. The generator can play solfeggio or masking tones at a chosen Hz for sound therapy, meditation, relaxation, focus or sleep routines, but this is not a medical device. Check your full audible range with a hearing test online — from 20Hz bass to 20kHz treble.

Tuning Instruments and Ear Training

Musicians know music tuning needs a steady reference: play 440 Hz and match your string. Ear training uses test tones to learn intervals, while harmonics show why an octave sounds so consistent.

Speaker, Subwoofer and Audio Equipment Testing

Sweep a subwoofer through sub-bass to find rattles, then climb to treble to check the tweeters. A sudden loud spot reveals a resonant frequency, a sign of resonance in the cabinet or room. Calibrating amplifiers and mixers, audio level alignment for radio, acoustics and equalization all rely on a steady signal, and calibration against a known level beats guessing. The stereo test plays audio through each channel independently to catch balance and routing issues.

Hz Generator Safety and Volume Limits

A hz generator can damage speakers and your ears at extreme levels, even with quiet tones, so safety comes first. Set a comfortable level on a mid-range tone and never go above it. The same caution applies to square and sawtooth waves, and to white noise or pink noise in desktop software. Lower the volume before you change frequency or waveform.

Frequently Asked Questions

What frequency range can an online tone generator play?

Most browser tone generators cover roughly 1 Hz to 20,000 Hz, which spans human hearing from deep sub-bass to the highest treble. How low you actually hear depends on your speakers or headphones, since small drivers cannot reproduce very low tones.

Is it safe to play high or low frequencies?

Extreme frequencies at high volume can damage your hearing and your speakers. Find a comfortable level on a mid-range tone, such as 1,000 Hz, and do not go far above it, even when you cannot hear much.

What are the different waveforms for?

A sine wave is a pure tone with no overtones and suits hearing and tuning tests. Square, sawtooth and triangle waves add overtones, giving a buzzier or brighter sound that is useful for checking speaker response.

Can I use a tone generator to tune an instrument?

Yes. Play a reference such as 440 Hz and match your string or pipe to it by ear. The Nearest Note result shows which note a frequency is closest to and how many cents sharp or flat it is.

How do I find my tinnitus frequency?

Move the frequency until a tone seems to match the ringing, then check one octave higher and one octave lower, because tones an octave apart are easy to confuse. This is not a medical device, so see a clinician for diagnosis.

How do I share a specific tone with someone?

Copy the Shareable Link Settings result and add it to the page address. It stores your frequency, waveform, volume and balance, so the other person opens the same tone.

Does a tone generator work offline?

Browser tools need the page loaded first. For fully offline use, install desktop tone generator software, which can also save a tone as a WAV file.
