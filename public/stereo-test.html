Run this stereo test to hear whether your left speaker and right speaker each play on the correct side, straight from your browser. Press Left, Center or Right and listen: the audio should follow the side you picked. In about a minute you will know whether your speakers, headphones or earbuds have reversed, silent or merged channels, and what to fix next.

How to run a left and right audio test on your speakers

Sit where you normally listen, face the speakers and follow these steps. This test doubles as a left and right speaker test for any laptop, phone or desktop audio setup. The free speaker test online works with any speakers or audio output device connected to your browser.

  1. Start low. Set the volume on your computer, output device or amplifier to a comfortable level, because test sounds can be harsher than music.
  2. Press Left. The sound should come only from the left speaker or the left headphone cup.
  3. Press Right. The same sound should move entirely to the right side.
  4. Press Center. With matching speakers, the sound should sit in the middle, not lean left or right.
  5. Use the pan sweep. The signal should glide smoothly from left to right with no jumps.

Keep the playback level steady between presses, so a difference you hear comes from the output and not from a louder tone.

This stereo sound test checks which side each signal reaches. It cannot prove polarity, but a centered voice that sounds thin suggests speakers pushing against each other. When both speakers are in phase, the center image is solid and bass is full; out of phase, it turns hollow. If only a single channel seems off, the sound itself is fine and the fault sits in the chain.

Fixing left/right stereo wiring that plays reversed

If Left plays right, first confirm you are facing the speakers. Then trace the cable from each output to its speaker: the left output must reach the left amplifier input. On an RCA cable, red is right and white is left, so check both ends of the connector. Bypass any virtual routing layer and compare a trusted recording in another player. If it plays wrong there too, correct the mapping you found, and never rewire powered hardware while it is live. A wired set-up with long speaker cables is the usual culprit; swapped plugs are the second, and a right channel on the left almost always traces to one of them. Wireless pairs rarely swap, so a reversed result there suggests a software setting.

Silent speaker or earbud: a quick stereo check

When one side makes no sound, open your system settings and make sure the balance is centered. Reseat the connector, remove adapters, and repeat this stereo check through a known-good output. That comparison shows whether the output path or the drivers inside one speaker have failed, because a fault that follows the device is not your output. For wiring you can reach safely, swap the two source cables once and replay Center: if the silence moves, suspect the source output.

Headphone test for earbuds and headsets

This headphone test uses the same buttons. If one earbud is quiet, clean its mesh, then wear the headphones the other way around. If the weak side follows the earbud, the unit is at fault. A headset on a phone can also fail when Bluetooth switches to call mode.

When stereo channels collapse to mono

If pressing Left plays through both sides, the route may be summing to mono. Look at your mono audio accessibility setting, a Bluetooth call profile, a single-speaker phone and any mixer between the browser and the speakers. Windows, macOS and iPhone each hide the switch under accessibility. Press Center to separate mono from a headset fault: with mono, Center still sounds normal, but a headset plug that misses its ground contact makes Center nearly vanish. Playing through both sides can also fool your hearing at low volume, so repeat it louder only if it is comfortable.

Stereo balance check when one side is quieter

A quieter side that still plays points to level, not routing. Move the balance slider to the middle, then swap earbuds or headphones around. Your hearing may differ between ears, and this audio test cannot tell that from the equipment.

How a browser stereo test routes the signal

The page builds a tone with the Web Audio API and passes it through a stereo panner running from -1 (full left) to +1 (full right). An equal-power pan keeps loudness steady while the signal moves:

g_L = cos(((p+1)π) / 4), g_R = sin(((p+1)π) / 4)

At Center (p = 0) both outputs carry about 0.707 of full level, at p = -0.5 the gains are 0.924 and 0.383. Your browser may block playback until you click, so press a button first.

Pink noise or a sine wave tone

A sine wave makes channel identity clear. Pink noise spreads energy across a wider band, so a level difference between sides is easier to notice. Neither is a calibrated measurement, and background noise in your room will still affect what you hear.

Stereo test with no microphone and no upload

The signal is generated locally. This page never asks for a microphone and never uploads your sound anywhere.

A leaning center image, traced with a left and right audio test

The decision on my desk is simple: do I buy new cables, or just move the chair? My bookshelf speakers sit 1.20 m apart and my chair is close to the tip of that triangle. I press Left, then Right, then Center, at a low level each time. Left plays left and Right plays right, so the wiring is fine. Yet Center clearly sits left of my monitor. The channel test has already ruled out reversed or missing sides, which leaves geometry and level.

I measure the distances with a tape: 1.20 m to the right speaker, 0.89 m to the left. That 0.31 m gap delays the right speaker by 0.90 ms (0.31 m ÷ 343 m/s = 0.0009 s). Sound that arrives first pulls the image toward its speaker, the precedence effect, so Center leans left even though both channels carry the same level.

My first instinct is the balance control, replaying the middle press after each change to see whether the image recentres. Shifting it toward the right turns the left channel down like this:

Balance shifted rightLeft channel gainLeft level change
0%1.000.00 dB
10%0.90-0.92 dB
22%0.78-2.16 dB
35%0.65-3.74 dB

At 22% the image recentres, but the left side is now 2.16 dB quieter, and a level gap of around 1 dB is often cited as audible on steady tones. That is a trade-off I do not want, so I reset balance to the middle. The number to watch here is the 0.31 m distance difference, not the decibels, because it fixes the cause rather than hiding it.

I slide the chair 0.31 m to the right and replay Center. The tone now sits between the speakers. The middle press exposed the lean, and the test's real job was ruling out a channel mix-up first. One caution: it only checks routing, so a pass says nothing about distance, and a balance slider cannot correct timing at all. If Center still drifts after the chairs are equal, I would compare a trusted stereo recording next, then check the room.

What this channel test cannot tell you about bass or frequency response

This is a routing check, not a measurement. It does not test frequency response, so it cannot show where a subwoofer rolls off or where small speakers lose bass. Deep bass and sub-bass need a separate frequency sweep, and a low-frequency buzz points to cabinet rattle rather than wiring. Check a subwoofer on its own, since its level and crossover sit outside this test. It also misses distortion and dynamic range limits, and it is not a hearing test or a calibration of your level. Surround sound speakers need their own channel checks, and good stereo separation depends on more than a pass here. Use a dedicated sweep to diagnose range, and keep troubleshooting one change at a time.

Speaker test placement for sharper stereo imaging

Passing the routing check is only the start. Place your loudspeakers an equal distance from your listening position, with the tweeters at ear height and angled inward, so stereophonic sound forms a clear soundstage and the audio feels anchored. Alan Blumlein showed in 1931 that two channels could imitate direction, which is why a centered image should land midway between the pair. Keep the speakers away from walls, since the room adds acoustic boom, and re-run the test after moving anything in a home theater setup.

Stereo audio test passes but the file is wrong

If this sound test routes correctly yet an exported WAV has a missing or swapped channel, inspect the file rather than your speakers. A known-good recording in another player settles the question, and troubleshooting the source file first saves swapping cables for nothing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a stereo test check?

It plays a tone on the left, right or center so you can confirm each channel reaches the correct speaker or earbud. It checks routing, not frequency response or hearing.

How do I know if my speakers are wired correctly?

Play the left channel: only the left speaker should sound. If the right one plays, your speaker cables or channel mapping are reversed, so swap them at the amplifier or in your audio settings.

Why does the left channel play through both sides?

Your output may be summing stereo to mono. Check mono-audio accessibility settings, a Bluetooth call profile, a single-speaker device and any mixer between the browser and the speakers.

Does the stereo test use my microphone?

No. The tone is generated in your browser and sent to your current audio output, with no microphone access and no upload.

What does the in phase and out of phase test tell me?

If the centered sound is solid with full bass in phase and thin or hollow out of phase, your speaker polarity is probably correct. If both sound the same, one speaker may be wired backward.

Why is one side quieter than the other?

A quieter side that still plays points to level, not routing. Center the balance slider, clean earbud mesh, then swap the headphones around to see whether the weak side follows the unit.

Can I use this test on headphones and phones?

Yes, it works with headphones, earbuds and phones. A phone's single built-in speaker cannot show stereo separation, so use wired or stereo Bluetooth headphones for a real check.
